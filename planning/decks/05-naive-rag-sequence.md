This companion doc maps the 13-slide explainer deck (`site/static/decks/05-naive-rag.html`) to the Module 5 lesson (`site/docs/m5-naive-rag/lesson.md`). The deck teaches **concepts**, not commands — it turns each of the lesson's analogies into a hand-drawn whiteboard visual a learner walks through before opening the lab. The visual language follows the CourseSmith whiteboard style contract (`coursesmith/templates/deck/whiteboard-style-guide.md`): Patrick Hand cursive, `#1e1e1e` primary / `#757575` secondary strokes on white paper with the five semantic pastel fills (§1), the `#rough` wobble filter on shapes only, and the shared `#ah`/`#ahg` arrowhead markers. The arc moves vocabulary forward — a raw LLM answers your ops questions confidently and wrongly → RAG hands it a cheat sheet at query time → the four-part anatomy of every GenAI app → the vector DB as a librarian shelving by meaning → embeddings as coordinates → the two-phase naive-RAG pipeline sharing one embedding step → where each piece runs on Apple Silicon → ChromaDB as the lightest store → env-var wiring in Compose → Learning Mode making the pipeline visible → the five predictable failure modes that motivate M6 → hand-off to the lab.

## Recommended presentation order

Present strictly 1 → 13; each slide's vocabulary is used by the next. Open on the title pipeline (1) and the five-objective preview (2), then land the motivating failure immediately: slide 3 (plausible-but-wrong command vs grounded answer) is the hinge that makes RAG worth building — linger here and read both panels aloud. Slide 4 names the four parts so the learner can place everything that follows. Slides 5–6 are one continuous analogy build (librarian → embeddings-as-coordinates); announce "shelved by meaning" at 5 and formalize it at 6 without breaking stride. Slide 7 is the centerpiece — walk the ingest row left to right, then the query row, then point at the dashed "same embedding model" link; that shared step is the single mechanic the lab's Learning Mode will prove. Slide 8 re-anchors the course's Apple-Silicon constraint (models native, everything else containers) — compress it if the audience has M2 fresh, never skip it. Slides 9–10 are the engineering-choice pair (which store, how it's wired); under time pressure compress 9's scale-up half but keep the 0.5.20 pin warning. Slide 11 previews the exact panel the learner will watch in the lab. Slide 12 is the bridge to Module 6 — read all five failure rows; they are the reason M6 exists. Slide 13 hands off to the lab — say the closing line and stop talking.
